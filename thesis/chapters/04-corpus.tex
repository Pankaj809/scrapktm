\chapter{Corpus Design and Statistics}
\label{ch:corpus}

\section{Document Selection Criteria}
Documents were selected to maximize thematic coverage and real-world utility for Kathmandu Valley residents. Priority was given to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item frequently accessed procedures (business registration, building permits, tax payment);
  \item financial schedules with numeric tables (property tax slabs, parking fees);
  \item federal acts governing local government operation~\cite{local_gov_act_2074}; and
  \item representation of both text-based and scanned PDFs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Extraction Performance}
Across all 50 documents, PyMuPDF extraction averaged 0.18 seconds per document. Scanned documents routed to Surya OCR incur higher latency proportional to page count; the pipeline supports \texttt{--ocr-max-pages} for development runs.

\section{Chunking Statistics}
The extraction pipeline produced \textbf{446 chunks} across 50 documents (mean 8.9 chunks per document). Documents with dense financial schedules produce the largest chunk counts:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \texttt{KTM\_FIN\_002}: 93 chunks (Kathmandu Economic Act, 2078)
  \item \texttt{KTM\_FIN\_001}: 66 chunks
  \item \texttt{BKT\_FIN\_003}: 43 chunks
\end{itemize}

\section{Data Artifacts}
The pipeline emits structured artifacts suitable for downstream RAG deployment:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \texttt{extraction\_output/text/<doc\_id>.txt} --- raw extracted text
  \item \texttt{extraction\_output/chunks/<doc\_id>\_chunks.jsonl} --- chunked JSONL
  \item \texttt{extraction\_output/extraction\_summary.jsonl} --- per-document metrics
  \item \texttt{curated\_corpus\_manifest.jsonl} --- corpus metadata
\end{itemize}
